About

Ivan Yuri De Leon writes under two names. As Lion, he researches AI safety and Filipino NLP. As Láyon, he publishes poetry in Filipino. But under these, he is just a single person with a single preoccupation: what happens when the systems that humans build become more capable than those who built them, and what that means for what it means to be human.

He took his Master's in Data Science at Ateneo de Manila University and graduated Summa Cum Laude in Applied Mathematics with a minor in Economics. Upon graduation, he received the Mulry Award, the university's highest distinction for literary excellence and service. He has also undertaken AI safety training through the TARA Program, conducting hands-on implementation work in technical AI alignment via ARENA's curriculum, covering transformers, mechanistic interpretability, reinforcement learning methods, and LLM evaluation.

He has gained academic experience through internships and research. He has gained industry experience through internships in data science and AI at Ayala Corporation, a major Philippine conglomerate, and BPI, a leading bank in the Philippines. He also worked as a research intern at the Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST) in Japan, collaborating with the Human–AI Interaction Laboratory on AI evaluation, and at Ateneo de Manila University, supporting work in graph theory.

His research sits at the intersection of AI and language. He currently works in technical AI alignment and Filipino language intellectualization. He also conducts LLM evaluations on issues relevant to consumer states like the Philippines. More broadly, he is interested in making increasingly capable AI systems reliable, beneficial, and aligned with human values.

Beyond his technical work, Lion remains deeply engaged with language and literature. During his undergraduate years, he served as Managing Editor for Finance at HEIGHTS Ateneo, the university's official literary publication, promoting Filipino literary work within a predominantly English-speaking academic environment. Currently, he still writes poems in Filipino as Láyon, tackling posthuman themes.

Strip away all of this, he knows that only one thing will remain with him: being human. And he fears that gradual disempowerment will one day sever him from this. While he studies and helps build intelligent systems, he is deeply concerned that their capabilities may one day exceed our own. This possibility motivates his interest in AI safety and alignment, not only as technical problems, but as human endeavors. Through both research and writing, he returns to the same question. How do we create powerful technologies without surrendering the values, relationships, and forms of meaning that make us human in the first place? He knows that in the world we currently inhabit, this effort is imperative. This principle guides everything he does. He builds carefully and thinks critically, hoping to ensure that increasingly intelligent systems remain in service of humanity rather than at its expense. So he combats his fear by amplifying what he knows best: being human.
