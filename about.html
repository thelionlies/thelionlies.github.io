About

Ivan Yuri "Lion" De Leon writes under two names. As Lion, he researches AI safety and Filipino NLP. As Láyon, he publishes poetry in Filipino. But he is just one person with one preoccupation: what happens when the systems we build become more capable than the people who built them, and what that means for what it means to be human.

He took his Master's in Data Science at Ateneo de Manila University and graduated Summa Cum Laude in Applied Mathematics with a minor in Economics. Upon graduation, he received the Mulry Award, the university's highest distinction for literary excellence and service. He has also undertaken AI safety training through the TARA Program, conducting hands-on implementation work in technical AI alignment via ARENA's curriculum, covering transformers, mechanistic interpretability, reinforcement learning methods, and LLM evaluation.

He has gained academic experience through internships and independent research. He worked as a research assistant at the Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST) in Japan, collaborating with the Human–AI Interaction Laboratory on AI evaluation, and at Ateneo de Manila University, supporting work in graph theory. He has likewise gained industry experience through internships at a leading local bank and a major Philippine conglomerate, working on projects ranging from predictive analytics to customer behavior modeling.

His research sits at the intersection of AI and language. He currently works in two areas: technical AI alignment and the intellectualization of Filipino. He also conducts LLM evaluations on issues relevant to consumer states like the Philippines. More broadly, he is interested in making increasingly capable AI systems reliable, beneficial, and aligned with human values.

Beyond his technical work, Lion remains deeply engaged with language and literature. During his undergraduate years, he served as Managing Editor for Finance at HEIGHTS Ateneo, the university's official literary publication, promoting Filipino literary work within a predominantly English-speaking academic environment. Currently, he still writes poems in Filipino as Láyon, tackling posthuman themes.

He fears that a gradual disempowerment will one day sever him from what he knows best: remaining human. He studies and helps build intelligent systems while recognizing that their capabilities may one day exceed our own. This possibility motivates his interest in AI safety and alignment, not only as technical problems, but as human ones. Through both research and writing, he returns to the same question: how do we create powerful technologies without surrendering the values, relationships, and forms of meaning that make us human in the first place? He knows that in the world we currently inhabit, this effort is imperative. This principle guides everything he does. He builds carefully and thinks critically, hoping to ensure that increasingly intelligent systems remain in service of humanity rather than at its expense. So he combats his fear by amplifying what he knows best: remaining human.
